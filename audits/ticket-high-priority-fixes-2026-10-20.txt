UPFORIT HIGH-PRIORITY TICKET FIXES — 20 OCTOBER 2026

Scope: F1 and F2 from ticket-pipeline-audit-2026-10-19.txt. The five medium-priority findings remain open.

F1: rejected email submissions
- Only an error thrown by the SendGrid submission call can be classified as a provider rejection; a later database error cannot masquerade as one.
- Explicit HTTP 429 responses become retryable failures with the existing exponential backoff and five-attempt ceiling. The sending marker is cleared so a later preparation failure is still safely retryable.
- Other explicit 4xx rejections (excluding 408) become terminal failed jobs by exhausting their retry budget. Provider validation details remain visible.
- Timeouts, network errors, HTTP 408/5xx gateway/server ambiguity, and failures recording an accepted submission remain unknown after sending starts. These require reconciliation rather than blind resending. Existing unknown jobs are not mass-requeued.
- Provider delivery/bounce callbacks cannot be overwritten by this failure handler.

F2: payments for unavailable events or closed sales
- Operational policy confirmed by Scott: expired events have no Buy Tickets button or purchase route; the button is removed the day after the event. The server-side safeguards cover already-open hosted checkouts, cancellation, and delayed payment notifications. No expired-event purchase route was introduced.
- Admission eligibility is checked inside fulfilment, while holding the order and event locks. Cancellation that commits before fulfilment's event lock is observed even when the two operations overlap.
- Cancelled/completed events, elapsed sales deadlines, and sales closed at the Stripe payment-event time route to refund_review with admissions_hold=true and a persisted reason.
- These orders receive an order number and retain their payment reference but generate no tickets, QR codes, merchandise entitlement or normal ticket-confirmation email.
- One encrypted payment-review notification is queued per order. Its wording acknowledges payment, says tickets cannot be issued, asks the buyer not to pay again, and does not claim that a refund has happened. The worker cancels a stale review notice if the review has already resolved.
- Repeated webhooks and later event reopening cannot silently issue tickets for an already-held closure payment. Refunds remain a deliberate reconciliation action; no automated financial refund was added.
- Stripe event time is persisted as paid_at. Migration 014 records event sales-state history using a database trigger, including closure/reopening. A delayed webhook for a payment made before an ordinary sales closure can fulfil; an already-cancelled event remains blocked regardless. Pre-migration history is not invented: if history cannot establish the state, current state is used conservatively. Stripe's second precision treats closure in the payment second as closed.
- Existing payment-adjustment/refund protections, signature verification, amount/currency correlation, and fulfilment deduplication are retained. Testing-only admin simulation retains its explicit rehearsal path.
